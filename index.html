Clay courts

Court

Between matches a clay court needs brick dust and a film of water, spread evenly, then left alone. Court meters the dust, lays the water, and returns to a dock to refill. The machines that do this today are priced for a handful of clubs. Court is the one a club can buy.

Streets

Drift

Most litter sits on a pavement until a crew has time for it. Drift is a tracked robot with an arm, a gripper, and a trailer. It picks the bottle up and puts it in the bin. The first season is driven from a phone. Every run is kept, so the next one needs less of a driver.

TrailerDump at the dock

Rivers

Current

Floating plastic gathers where the water slows, against a bank or below a bridge. Current runs a conveyor at the surface, holds what it takes, and writes down the path. A cleanup you can point to later, not a photograph of one.

RecordPath and load

Air, Belgrade

Plume

A bad air day is usually blamed on a district. Plume flies the plume back to the chimney it came from and brings back video, a position, the parcel, and a sample. An inspector decides what happens next. TRACE does not issue the fine.

PinThe stack
SampleFor the inspector

Water

Gauge

A river sampled once is a story. Gauge leaves a buoy in the channel and a small station on the bank, and takes the same measurements on a schedule. The other machines report against that record. A change shows up as a number, not a visit three days later.
